Curriculum

This is the catalogue. If you would rather be guided, the learning path puts every course and lesson in one recommended order with progress tracking. A free university curriculum in computational materials science. It starts at first-year level, so that a new university student can begin here, and runs up to graduate research methods. Each course teaches the concepts it needs, includes worked examples and problems with solutions, and lists the courses it builds on.

How the courses are numbered

Course codes follow the usual university pattern: a subject, then a three-digit number whose first digit is the level.

  • Subject: MATH mathematics; PHYS physics; MSE materials science and engineering; COMP computing and numerical methods; CMS computational materials science.
  • Level (first digit): 1 Year 1; 2 Year 2; 3 Year 3; 4 Year 4; 5 graduate. Doctoral-level courses (7) are planned for later.

Within a subject, work through the courses in number order; across subjects, the Needs column says what to have finished first. Year 1 courses assume only school mathematics and science. A course numbered 100 (such as MATH 100) is a guide to its subject rather than a lecture course.

Where to start

  • New to university science. Begin with Year 1: MATH 101 and MATH 103 together, with MSE 101 and COMP 101. The mathematics guide has a short placement check.
  • You have a science or engineering degree. Use the placement check in the mathematics guide to skip what you know, then take the Year 2 and Year 3 courses you are missing and move on to Year 4 and the graduate courses.
  • You want a particular method. Open its graduate course (CMS 5xx) and follow the Needs links backwards, or use the prerequisite map.
