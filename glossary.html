Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the words used across the site. Terms in the topic pages are underlined with a dotted line: hover, focus or tap one to see its meaning without leaving the page.

A B C D E F G H I K L M N O P R S T U V W Y

A

acceleration
The rate at which velocity changes. Read more →
activation energy
The energy barrier a process must overcome to happen. Read more →
active learning
Letting a model choose which calculation or experiment to do next, to learn as efficiently as possible. Read more →
Allen-Cahn
The phase-field equation for fields such as grain orientation that are not conserved; it moves interfaces by curvature. Read more →
alloy
A metal made by mixing two or more elements. Read more →
amorphous
Without long-range atomic order, as in glass. Read more →
Arrhenius law
The rule that a thermally activated rate grows as exp(-Q/kT) with temperature. Read more →
atom
The smallest unit of an element: a tiny nucleus surrounded by a cloud of electrons, about 0.1 to 0.3 nanometres across. Read more →
atomic number
The number of protons in an atom's nucleus; it identifies the element. Read more →
Avogadro number
6.022 x 10^23, the number of items in one mole. Read more →

B

band gap
The range of energies with no electron states, which makes a material a semiconductor or insulator. Read more →
barostat
An algorithm that keeps a simulation at a chosen pressure. Read more →
Bloch's theorem
The result that electron states in a periodic crystal are plane waves modulated by the lattice periodicity. Read more →
Boltzmann constant
The constant k_B = 8.617 x 10^-5 eV/K that converts temperature into energy. Read more →
Boltzmann distribution
The probability that a system at temperature T is in a state of energy E, proportional to exp(-E/kT). Read more →
bond
The attraction that holds atoms together, arising from the electrons and nuclei of neighbouring atoms. Read more →
bond energy
The energy needed to separate two bonded atoms; the depth of the energy valley. Read more →
bond length
The distance between two bonded atoms at which their energy is lowest. Read more →
Born-Oppenheimer approximation
Treating the nuclei as fixed while the much lighter electrons adjust instantly. Read more →
Brillouin zone
The basic cell of reciprocal space in which electron and phonon wavevectors are labelled. Read more →
brittle
Breaking with little permanent deformation. Read more →
Burgers vector
The vector that measures the size and direction of the lattice distortion of a dislocation. Read more →

C

Cahn-Hilliard
The phase-field equation for conserved fields such as composition; it describes spinodal decomposition. Read more →
CALPHAD
A method that models the free energy of each phase and calculates phase diagrams and equilibria for alloys. Read more →
central limit theorem
The result that sums or averages of many independent random effects tend to a bell-shaped distribution. Read more →
ceramic
A hard, brittle, heat-resistant material, usually a compound of metal and non-metal atoms. Read more →
chemical potential
The change in free energy when one particle is added; equal in all phases at equilibrium. Read more →
cluster expansion
A way of writing the energy of any arrangement of atoms on a lattice as a sum over small clusters of sites. Read more →
common tangent
The straight line touching a free-energy curve at two points, which gives the compositions of coexisting phases. Read more →
composite
A material combining two or more constituents to get properties neither has alone. Read more →
condition number
A measure of how much a problem amplifies small errors in its input. Read more →
convergence
The result settling to a stable value as a numerical setting (step size, mesh, sample size) is improved. Read more →
covalent bond
A bond in which neighbouring atoms share pairs of electrons in definite directions. Read more →
creep
Slow permanent deformation of a material under constant load at high temperature. Read more →
cross-validation
Testing a model on data held out from training, repeated over several splits. Read more →
crystal
A solid in which atoms sit in a regular pattern that repeats in all directions. Read more →

D

defect
Any departure from a perfect crystal: vacancies, solutes, dislocations, boundaries and so on. Read more →
density functional theory
A method that calculates the energy and electronic structure of a material from the electron density. Read more →
density of states
The number of available states per unit energy. Read more →
derivative
The slope of a curve at a point: the instantaneous rate of change. Read more →
descriptor
A set of numbers that describes a material or atomic environment for use in a machine-learning model. Read more →
detailed balance
The condition that the flow between two states is the same in both directions at equilibrium. Read more →
differential equation
An equation that relates a quantity to its rate of change. Read more →
diffraction
The pattern formed when waves scatter from the regular planes of atoms in a crystal. Read more →
diffusion
The spreading of atoms or molecules by random motion. Read more →
diffusion coefficient
A number that sets how fast diffusion happens; MSD = 2Dt in one dimension. Read more →
dislocation
A line defect in a crystal, like an extra half-sheet of atoms, whose motion lets metals deform permanently. Read more →
ductile
Able to deform a lot permanently before breaking. Read more →

E

eigenvalue
A number by which a matrix stretches one of its special directions (eigenvectors); energies and vibration frequencies are eigenvalues. Read more →
eigenvector
A direction that a matrix only stretches, without turning it. Read more →
electron
A very light, negatively charged particle in the cloud around the nucleus; electrons are what bond atoms together. Read more →
electronvolt
A unit of energy equal to 1.602 x 10^-19 J, convenient for atomic-scale energies. Read more →
element
A kind of atom defined by its number of protons, such as iron or carbon. Read more →
ensemble
The set of states a system can be in under given conditions (fixed temperature, volume and so on). Read more →
entropy
A measure of how many microscopic arrangements correspond to a given state; systems tend toward higher entropy. Read more →
equilibrium
A state with no net force, or in which nothing changes on average. Read more →
equipartition
The rule that each quadratic term in the energy carries, on average, half of k_B T. Read more →
ergodic
Visiting all accessible states in proportion to their probability, so time averages equal ensemble averages. Read more →
exchange-correlation
The part of the electron-electron interaction that DFT must approximate. Read more →
exponential
A function of the form A e^(kx), which grows or decays by a fixed factor over equal steps. Read more →

F

fatigue
Failure of a material under repeated loading at stresses well below its yield strength. Read more →
Fermi energy
The highest occupied electron energy in a metal at zero temperature. Read more →
Fick's laws
The laws stating that diffusive flux is proportional to the concentration gradient and that concentration changes accordingly. Read more →
finite element method
A numerical method that divides a body into small elements and solves a differential equation on them. Read more →
floating point
The way computers store real numbers with a fixed number of significant digits. Read more →
force
A push or pull that changes the motion of an object; measured in newtons. Read more →
Fourier transform
A way of writing a signal as a sum of waves of different wavelengths. Read more →
fracture toughness
A measure of how well a material resists the growth of a crack. Read more →
free energy
The quantity, combining energy and entropy, that is lowest at equilibrium at fixed temperature. Read more →

G

git
A tool that records the history of a project so that you can go back and collaborate. Read more →
glass
A solid with no repeating atomic pattern, like a frozen liquid. Read more →
gradient
An arrow pointing in the direction of steepest increase of a quantity, with length equal to the steepness. Read more →
grain
A single small crystal inside a polycrystalline material. Read more →
grain boundary
The surface where two grains of different orientation meet. Read more →

H

half-life
The time for a decaying quantity to fall to half its value. Read more →
Hall-Petch relation
The rule that a metal gets stronger as its grain size gets smaller. Read more →
heat
Energy transferred between bodies because of a temperature difference. Read more →
histogram
A bar chart showing how many data values fall into each range. Read more →
Hooke's law
The rule that a spring's restoring force is proportional to its stretch, F = -kx. Read more →

I

integral
The area under a curve; the sum of many thin slices. Read more →
interatomic potential
A function giving the energy of a set of atoms from their positions, used to calculate forces. Read more →
interstitial
An atom squeezed into the space between the normal sites of a crystal. Read more →
ionic bond
A bond in which one atom hands electrons to another and the resulting opposite charges attract. Read more →
Ising model
A lattice of up and down spins that prefer to align with neighbours; the simplest model of a phase transition. Read more →

K

k-points
Sample points in the Brillouin zone used for integrals in electronic-structure calculations. Read more →
kelvin
The unit of absolute temperature; 0 K is absolute zero and K = degrees Celsius + 273.15. Read more →
kinetic energy
Energy of motion, equal to half the mass times the speed squared. Read more →
kinetic Monte Carlo
A method that picks real events (such as atom hops) with their rates and advances physical time. Read more →

L

lattice
The regular array of points on which the atoms of a crystal are arranged. Read more →
least squares
Fitting a line or curve by minimising the sum of the squared distances to the data points. Read more →
lever rule
The rule that gives the fractions of two coexisting phases from the overall composition. Read more →
logarithm
The power to which a base must be raised to give a number; log10(1000) = 3. Read more →

M

machine learning
Fitting flexible functions to data so that they can predict new cases. Read more →
martensite
A very hard steel phase formed by rapid cooling, through a shear-like change of the lattice without atoms diffusing. Read more →
matrix
A rectangular table of numbers that represents a linear transformation. Read more →
mesh
The division of a body into small elements used in finite element and similar methods. Read more →
metallic bond
A bond in which outer electrons are shared by all atoms and move freely, giving metals their conductivity and ductility. Read more →
Metropolis algorithm
The rule that accepts a move if it lowers the energy, or otherwise with probability exp(-dE/kT). Read more →
microstructure
The arrangement of grains, phases, particles and defects seen at micrometre scale. Read more →
Miller indices
Small integers that label directions and planes in a crystal. Read more →
miscibility gap
A range of composition and temperature in which a solution separates into two phases. Read more →
model
A simplified description of a system that keeps what matters and ignores the rest. Read more →
mole
A counting unit equal to 6.022 x 10^23 items, used to count atoms and molecules. Read more →
molecular dynamics
A simulation that follows atoms by calculating forces and integrating Newton's equations in small time steps. Read more →
Monte Carlo
A family of methods that sample states by random moves accepted or rejected with set probabilities. Read more →
MPI
A standard for programs that run on many processors by passing messages. Read more →

N

neutron
An uncharged particle in the nucleus with nearly the same mass as a proton. Read more →
Newton's laws
Three laws of motion: objects keep their velocity unless a force acts, F = ma, and forces come in equal and opposite pairs. Read more →
normal distribution
The bell-shaped curve that describes many random errors and averages. Read more →
nucleation
The first formation of a tiny region of a new phase. Read more →
nucleus
The tiny, dense core of an atom, made of protons and neutrons, holding almost all of its mass. Read more →
NumPy
A Python library for fast calculations with arrays of numbers. Read more →

O

order of magnitude
A factor of ten; two numbers differ by one order of magnitude if one is about ten times the other. Read more →
order parameter
A field that is different in different phases or grains, such as +1 in one phase and -1 in another. Read more →
overfitting
A model fitting the noise in its training data so closely that it predicts new data badly. Read more →

P

partition function
The sum of Boltzmann factors over all states, from which free energy and averages follow. Read more →
Pauli exclusion principle
No two electrons can occupy the same quantum state. Read more →
periodic boundary conditions
A simulation trick in which the box repeats in all directions, so an atom leaving one side re-enters on the other. Read more →
periodic table
The chart of elements ordered by atomic number, with similar elements in the same column. Read more →
phase
A region of material with uniform structure and composition, such as the solid or the liquid. Read more →
phase diagram
A map showing which phases are stable at each temperature and composition. Read more →
phase-field
A method that describes microstructure with smooth fields so that moving interfaces need no special tracking. Read more →
phonon
A quantised vibration of the atoms of a crystal. Read more →
polycrystal
A solid made of many small crystals (grains) with different orientations. Read more →
polymer
A material made of long chain molecules, such as polyethylene or nylon. Read more →
potential energy
Energy stored because of position or arrangement, such as in a stretched spring or a chemical bond. Read more →
potential energy surface
The energy of a system of atoms as a function of all their positions; atoms move over it like balls over hills and valleys. Read more →
probability
A number from 0 to 1 saying how likely an event is. Read more →
proton
A positively charged particle in the nucleus; the number of protons decides which element an atom is. Read more →
pseudopotential
A replacement for the nucleus and core electrons so that only valence electrons are treated explicitly. Read more →
Python
A readable programming language widely used in science. Read more →

R

random walk
A path made of random steps, such as a coin-flip left or right each step; its distance grows as the square root of the number of steps. Read more →
reciprocal lattice
The lattice of wave patterns that fit the crystal; it describes diffraction and electron states. Read more →

S

Schrodinger equation
The basic equation of quantum mechanics, whose solutions give the allowed energies and states of electrons. Read more →
scientific notation
Writing a number as a value between 1 and 10 times a power of ten, such as 6.0 x 10^23. Read more →
self-consistent field
An iteration in which the electron density and the potential it creates are repeated until they agree. Read more →
semiconductor
A material that conducts electricity only a little, in a way that can be controlled, as silicon does. Read more →
significant figures
The digits of a number that carry real information. Read more →
simulation
The step-by-step calculation of what a model predicts. Read more →
slope
How much a line or curve rises for each step along; the rate of change. Read more →
Slurm
A common job scheduler for computer clusters. Read more →
solute
An atom of a minority element dissolved in a host material. Read more →
spinodal decomposition
Spontaneous separation of a solution into two phases, with no barrier to start it. Read more →
standard deviation
A measure of how spread out data are around their mean. Read more →
standard error
The uncertainty of an average: the standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of samples. Read more →
stiffness
How strongly something resists being stretched or squeezed. Read more →
strain
The stretch of a material as a fraction of its original length. Read more →
strain hardening
The increase of a metal's strength as it is deformed, caused by dislocations multiplying and obstructing each other. Read more →
stress
Force per unit area acting inside a material. Read more →
supercell
A larger cell made by repeating a unit cell, used so that a defect has room to sit in a simulation. Read more →

T

temperature
A measure of the average kinetic energy of the random motion of atoms. Read more →
tensor
A generalisation of vectors; stress and strain are tensors with two indices. Read more →
texture
A preferred orientation of the grains in a polycrystal, which makes properties depend on direction. Read more →
thermal energy
The energy of random atomic motion, of order k_B T per atom. Read more →
thermostat
An algorithm that keeps a simulation at a chosen temperature. Read more →
time step
The small interval of time by which a simulation advances at each step. Read more →

U

unit cell
The smallest repeating block of a crystal pattern. Read more →

V

vacancy
An empty atom site in a crystal. Read more →
valence electrons
The outermost electrons of an atom, which take part in bonding. Read more →
validation
Checking that the model agrees with independent experiments. Read more →
van der Waals
A weak attraction between neutral atoms or molecules caused by fleeting shifts of charge. Read more →
variational principle
Any trial wavefunction gives an energy at least as high as the true ground-state energy. Read more →
vector
A quantity with both size and direction, drawn as an arrow, such as a force or velocity. Read more →
velocity
How fast and in which direction something moves. Read more →
velocity-Verlet
A standard, accurate and stable method for stepping atoms forward in time in molecular dynamics. Read more →
verification
Checking that the equations are solved correctly. Read more →

W

wavefunction
The quantum description of a particle; its square gives the probability of finding it at a place. Read more →

Y

yield strength
The stress at which a material starts to deform permanently. Read more →
Young's modulus
The stiffness of a material: the ratio of stress to strain in the elastic region. Read more →

A reference series on computational materials science for materials scientists and metallurgists.
